# Choosing between contemporaneous and dynamic kernels

MCMSEM offers two moment kernels. Neither is a generally superior version of
the other. The choice states where causal time lives in the model.

## Public API and migration

```r
MCMmodel(ds, kernel = "contemporaneous") # default
MCMmodel(ds, kernel = "dynamic")
```

The former `kernel = "static"` spelling is a deprecated alias for
`"contemporaneous"`. It warns because "static" can misleadingly suggest that a
dynamic model is intrinsically preferable. Kernel names are matched exactly;
partial spellings are rejected.

## Contemporaneous Structural MCMSEM

Contemporaneous Structural MCMSEM assumes that measured variables can be
represented by structural equations at one conceptual occasion, such as
`Y = beta X + epsilon_Y`. The coefficient is interpreted interventionally:
replacing the structural equation for `X` by `X = x` changes the value generated
for `Y`. The model contains no explicit transition from `t` to `t + 1`. Prior
history, adaptation, feedback, and equilibrium processes are outside the model
and are absorbed into the variables, disturbances, or interpretation of the
coefficient. Higher-order moments may identify the direction of a
contemporaneous structural relation, but they do not create a temporal order
that the model does not contain.

Choosing this kernel assumes that a same-occasion structural intervention is
scientifically meaningful at the variables' measurement scale. It is most
defensible when one variable plausibly acts before another within the
measurement window, or when the path is interpreted as an equilibrium or
integrated contemporaneous response. If substantial reciprocal dynamics occur
inside the measurement interval, a contemporaneous path may summarize those
integrated dynamics rather than represent a single mechanistic transition.

The second-order propagation remains

```text
M2 = Fm (I - A)^-1 S (I - A)^-T Fm^T
```

with the existing third- and fourth-order propagation unchanged.

## Stationary Dynamic MCMSEM

Stationary Dynamic MCMSEM assumes that the system evolves through repeated,
time-homogeneous transitions,

```text
z[t] = B z[t-1] + epsilon[t].
```

An entry such as `beta_X_to_Y` represents the effect of `X[t]` on `Y[t+1]` at
the chosen lag. Although the model may be fitted to one cross-sectional
stationary distribution, time has not been conceptually integrated out.
Stationarity specifies how the unobserved history accumulates into that
distribution:

```text
z[t] = epsilon[t] + B epsilon[t-1] + B^2 epsilon[t-2] + ... .
```

For cumulant order `r`, the production kernel solves

```text
C_r = (I - B^(x r))^-1 D_r,
```

where `B^(x r)` denotes the r-fold Kronecker power. The package uses linear
solves, not explicit inverses. `D2` is fixed to the identity. `D3` and `D4` have
one freely estimated diagonal innovation cumulant per state and zero mixed
innovation cumulants. These restrictions encode the fixed innovation scale and
mutual independence assumptions.

Choosing this kernel commits the analyst to a temporal resolution and to
repeated stable transitions: the same `B` and innovation distribution operate
across time, innovations are serially independent, and the process has reached
stationarity. A one-day transition and a one-year transition are not the same
parameter. The dynamic model is mechanistically more explicit, but relies on
stronger assumptions about lag structure, stationarity, and omitted
intermediate dynamics.

## Gaussian residual or random-intercept covariance

The observed state may contain an independent Gaussian component:

```text
y[t] = z[t] + G,   G ~ N(0, Psi_G).
```

There is one component per trait, loading one on that trait, and `Psi_G` may be
full. MCMSEM parameterizes it as `L_G L_G^T`, with exponentiated Cholesky
diagonals, so every optimizer state produces a positive-definite covariance.
This component changes total covariance but contributes no third or fourth
cumulant. Since MCMSEM fits raw fourth central moments, the package reconstructs

```text
M4[i,j,k,l] = K4[i,j,k,l]
             + Sigma[i,j] Sigma[k,l]
             + Sigma[i,k] Sigma[j,l]
             + Sigma[i,l] Sigma[j,k],
```

using the total covariance `Sigma = C2 + Psi_G`.

## WLS weighting and asymptotic standard errors

The fitted vector contains unique raw central moments through order four. With
residual `r = s - sigma(theta)`, the dynamic quadratic criterion is `r' W r`.
`MCMdatasummary(..., prep_asymptotic_se = TRUE)` prepares
`Omega = Var(s)` using central-moment influence functions that account for the
estimated sample means.

`moment_weighting` may be `"identity"`, `"diagonal"` (inverse marginal moment
variances), or `"full"` (a regularized inverse of `Omega`). If `Delta` is the
Jacobian of the implied moments, identity and diagonal weighting default to
the robust sandwich covariance

```text
(Delta' W Delta)^-1 Delta' W Omega W Delta (Delta' W Delta)^-1.
```

Full WLS defaults to the efficient covariance `(Delta' W Delta)^-1`.
`se_correction = "robust"` can request the sandwich with any weight matrix.
Because `Omega` is stored as `Var(s)`, its `1/N` scaling is already present.
The reported SEs for the derived Gaussian covariance use a delta-method
Jacobian of `Psi_G = L_G L_G'`.

These are local unconstrained Wald approximations. Inspect Jacobian rank and the
reported information condition number, and be cautious when stationarity or
parameter bounds are active. The bundled 50-replication calibration shows close
ASE/empirical-SD agreement for an eight-parameter overidentified model, but
strongly conservative SEs for the weakly identified 11-parameter bivariate
model with a free Gaussian covariance (`inst/validation/README.md`).

## Identification and diagnostics

With two variables, unique moments through order four number `3 + 4 + 5 = 12`.
The full dynamic model estimates four entries of `B`, two third cumulants, two
fourth cumulants, and three Gaussian covariance parameters: 11 parameters and
one nominal overidentifying degree of freedom.

Counting is not a proof of global identification. Generic identification
depends on useful and nondegenerate non-Gaussian innovation cumulants, mutual
independence, fixed innovation scale, stationarity, distinct source cumulant
profiles, and the absence of unmodelled non-Gaussian confounding. Similar or
near-Gaussian sources can yield weak identification or several low-loss states.
Use multiple starts and inspect all-start diagnostics. `MCMdiagnostics(fit,
jacobian = TRUE)` adds a numerical Jacobian-rank check at the fitted solution.

The mathematical basis is discussed by Cecilie Olesen Recke, Sarah Lumpp,
Nataliia Kushnerchuk, Janike Oldekop, Jiayi Li, Jane Ivy Coons, and Elina Robeva
(2026), [Identifiability in Graphical Discrete Lyapunov
Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21818), arXiv:2601.21818. The cited record is a
2026 arXiv preprint; no journal publication status is claimed here.

## Example

```r
ds <- MCMdatasummary(
  dat[, c("X", "Y")],
  scale_data = FALSE,
  use_skewness = TRUE,
  use_kurtosis = TRUE
)

model <- MCMmodel(ds, kernel = "dynamic")
model <- MCMedit(model, "B", c(1, 1), "phi_X")
model <- MCMedit(model, "B", c(1, 2), "Y_lag_to_X")
model <- MCMedit(model, "B", c(2, 1), "X_lag_to_Y")
model <- MCMedit(model, "B", c(2, 2), "phi_Y")
model <- MCMedit(model, "lbound", "phi_X", 0)
model <- MCMedit(model, "lbound", "phi_Y", 0)

fit <- MCMfit(
  model, ds,
  compute_se = TRUE,
  moment_weighting = "diagonal",
  n_starts = 5,
  seed = 2026
)
summary(fit)
```

Fixed unit innovation variances make scale part of the identifying model.
`scale_data = FALSE` preserves the supplied measurement scale. Scaling remains
available, but changes the target parameterization.

## Current support and limitations

The dynamic kernel currently supports:

- two or more observed variables;
- a stationary VAR(1);
- independent innovations with fixed unit variances;
- diagonal innovation third and fourth cumulants;
- an optional full Gaussian residual covariance;
- covariance, co-skewness, and raw co-kurtosis;
- identity, diagonal, or full WLS moment weighting and asymptotic robust or
  efficient SEs;
- torch autograd, CPU/GPU device selection, bounds, multi-start fitting, common
  result objects, model comparison, summaries, and plots.

Latent-state measurement models are not yet implemented. Dynamic WLS and SEs
currently exclude analysis weights and require mean-corrected moment covariance
metadata produced by MCMSEM 0.27.0 or later. The Kronecker system has dimension
`p^r`, so fourth-order models become expensive as the number of states grows.

## Continuous time and future models

Both kernels can approximate richer continuous-time systems. One such model is

```text
dz(t) = A z(t) dt + dL(t),
```

where `A` contains rates of change and `L(t)` supplies Gaussian or non-Gaussian
shocks. At observation interval `Delta`, the discrete transition is
`B(Delta) = exp(A Delta)`. Discrete cross-lagged effects therefore depend on the
sampling interval and may combine direct and indirect evolution between
observations.

Natural future extensions include continuous-time cumulant kernels, VAR(q),
models with both contemporaneous and lagged paths, nonlinear or regime-switching
dynamics, nonstationary transitions, latent state/measurement-error models,
non-Gaussian confounders, and interventions defined over trajectories rather
than single time points.
